# Casual labour and the law

Shap's **Workers** record day labour: who worked which days, on which job, at what day rate, and
what they were paid (cash, an advance, savings held for them). Plenty of small South African
businesses pay helpers by the day like this. The law mostly treats those helpers as **employees**,
however few days they work, and what it asks is the business's to do, not Shap's. Shap records the
days and the pay; it doesn't decide who is an employee, and doesn't work out, deduct or pay UIF,
PAYE (employees' tax) or the Compensation Fund for a worker. For employees on **Payroll**, Shap
works out PAYE, UIF and SDL and prepares the EMP201 and EMP501 figures, as aids: filing and paying
them is the business's.

Shap is free software from OpenWorks IT, who are not accountants, tax practitioners or labour
lawyers. This page says where the law comes in so the owner knows to look; it isn't advice.
Check how you employ and pay your workers with your accountant or a labour practitioner.

## What the law says

| Law | What it means for day labour | Source |
|---|---|---|
| National Minimum Wage Act | Every worker, from the first hour: R30.23 an ordinary hour from 1 March 2026 (R28.79 from 1 March 2025). A bargaining council's or sectoral minimum can be higher, never lower; workers on an Expanded Public Works Programme and learners on a learnership have their own rates. Allowances (transport, food) don't count towards it | [GN R.7083, Government Gazette 54075 (3 February 2026)](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202602/54075rg11941gon7083.pdf), [the Minister's statement for 2025](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-nomakhosazana-meth-adjusts-national-minimum-wage-r2879-hour-05-feb), [the Act (s4, s5)](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/act/2018/9/eng@2026-03-01) |
| Income Tax Act, Fourth Schedule (PAYE) | Day wages are remuneration. Someone working at least 22 hours a week for one employer is in standard employment, and so (deemed) is someone working at least 5 hours a day for less than R394 a day (2026/27), or under 22 hours a week who gives a written declaration that they work for no other employer: SARS's tables apply, and a low wage may come to no tax. Anyone else: a flat 25% (unless SARS has issued a directive). An employer with an employee liable for tax registers for PAYE | [SARS: Guide for employers in respect of employees' tax (2027)](https://www.sars.gov.za/paye-gen-01-g21-guide-for-employers-iro-employees-tax-for-2027-external-guide/) |
| Unemployment Insurance Contributions Act | UIF applies to everyone employed by an employer for **24 hours a month or more** (s4(1)(a)): 1% from the worker and 1% from the employer (s6), deducted from their pay (s7) and paid monthly: to SARS with the EMP201 (s8), or, for an employer not required to register for PAYE, not registered voluntarily and not liable for SDL, to the UIF itself (s9). The employer registers (s10) | [UICA s4 (Acts Online)](https://source.acts.co.za/unemployment-insurance-contributions-act-2002/4__application_of_act.php), [UICA (SAFLII)](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/uica2002443) |
| Basic Conditions of Employment Act | From 24 hours a month with an employer: the chapters on working time (s6(1)(c)) and leave (s19(1)). The rest applies to employees generally: written particulars (s29), records of each employee's name, occupation, time worked and pay, kept three years (s31), and the written pay details on each pay day (s33). Under four hours on a day is paid as four (s9A). Up to 9 ordinary hours a day on a five-day week (s9) | [s6](https://source.acts.co.za/basic-conditions-of-employment-act-1997/bcoe97_6__application_of_this_chapter.php), [s19](https://source.acts.co.za/basic-conditions-of-employment-act-1997/bcoe97_19__application_of_this_chapter.php), [s31](https://source.acts.co.za/basic-conditions-of-employment-act-1997/bcoe97_31__keeping_of_records.php), [s9A](https://source.acts.co.za/basic-conditions-of-employment-act-1997/bcoe97_9a__daily_wage_payment.php) |
| Labour Relations Act, Employment Equity Act | Someone under 24 hours a month is still an employee for the LRA's protections (unfair dismissal) and the EEA. For someone earning at or under the BCEA earnings threshold (R269 900.90 a year from 1 May 2026): a fixed-term arrangement beyond three months needs a justifiable reason, or it's deemed permanent (s198B: not for employers with fewer than 10 employees, or fewer than 50 in their first two years unless the business came from dividing another), and a part-time employee may not be treated less favourably than a full-time one (s198C: not for someone working under 24 hours a month, or in their first three months). s200A presumes someone is an employee where, among others, the employer controls their work or hours, or they've worked 40 hours a month or more over the last three months | [LRA Chapter 9 (ss198A–198D, 200A)](https://worklaw.co.za/SearchDirectory/Legislation/LRACH9.ASP), [BCEA earnings threshold 2026](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/04/bcea-earnings-threshold-increase-2026) |
| Skills Development Levies Act | Day wages count with salaries towards the R500 000 a year above which SDL (1%) is due | [SARS: SDL](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/skills-development-levy/) |
| COIDA | A casual employee employed for the employer's business is an employee for the Compensation Fund, with no hours test: an employer with one or more employees registers, and their wages go on the return of earnings | [COIDA Amendment Act 10 of 2022](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202304/48431-17-4-compensationoccupinjuriesdiseasesamendact10of2022a.pdf), [who must register](https://www.eohcb.co.za/post/coida-who-must-register-for-coida) |
| Subcontractors | Someone who runs their own business and invoices you is a contractor, not an employee. Where you control how, when and where they work, SARS (Interpretation Note 17) and the labour laws (LRA s200A) can see them as your employee | [SARS Interpretation Note 17, Issue 5](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-intr-in-2012-17-employees-tax-independent-contractors) |
| Tax records | Records for tax (PAYE, UIF and SDL among them) are kept five years | [Tax Administration Act s29](https://api.acts.co.za/tax-administration-act-2011/29__duty_to_keep_records.php) |

## What Shap does about it

- **Workers** reminds you where the law comes in, from the days logged: about 24 hours logged in a
  month or more (this month or last, whichever is more; a day logged counts as 8 hours: Shap logs
  days, not hours), and a day rate under the national minimum wage for an 8-hour day. The
  worker's page marks each month they reached about 24 hours. These are reminders worked out from
  what's logged, not a decision about anyone, and not advice.
- The same note is on the workers list, the worker's page and the form for adding a worker; setup
  says it when it asks about casual labour, and again among the cautions before the terms.
- A subcontractor gets the independent contractor note instead, and isn't counted in the
  statements' employee costs.
- Payroll's EMP201 says when a month has day wages it doesn't include. Home warns when salaries and
  day wages together over the last 12 months pass R500 000 with SDL off.
- The financial statements' checks remind the accountant of leave pay, and of UIF, PAYE and the
  Compensation Fund on day wages, in a year with day labour of about 24 hours a month.
- Someone who works for the business regularly may belong on **Payroll** (ask your accountant),
  where Shap works out PAYE, UIF and SDL for the EMP201 you file.
- The days, rates and what was paid and when can be part of what the BCEA asks an employer to keep
  (s31), but not all of it: Shap logs days, not hours or occupations, and doesn't give a worker the
  written pay details the BCEA asks for on each pay day (s33).

Shap doesn't decide whether someone is an employee, doesn't deduct UIF or PAYE from day wages, and
doesn't file or pay anything for them. The national minimum wage and the deemed standard
employment day pay are in `packages/shared/src/labour.ts`, checked each year with the SARS tables
(CPDAUDIT.md section 2).
